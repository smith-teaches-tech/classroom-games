CAS · Class 2 · Evidence & Reflection

Prove It — evidence you actually grew

A photo proves you showed up. It can't prove you changed. Today we separate the two — then build a whole CAS experience, start to finish, the way it actually earns an outcome.

By the end of today: I can tell evidence of presence from evidence of growth, and I can plan an experience as a thread over time — repeated reflection, culminating in a final reflection.

00 / THE EVIDENCE DETECTIVE

Sam's juggling pile — what actually counts?

Sam is learning to juggle for the talent show and uploaded all this as "evidence." For each exhibit, commit first: does it prove Sam learned something, or just that Sam was there? Then flip it.

Presence — you were there Growth — you changed
Evidence proves you were there. Reflection proves you changed. CAS grades the change — so your pile needs growth in it, not just attendance.
01 / BUILD ONE PROPERLY

An experience is a thread, not a moment

Now we build a full experience end to end. Follow Sam's juggling on the board — then switch to Make it mine and plan your own.

Mode:

Claim it

What are you doing, which strand(s) does it live in, which outcome(s) will it grow — and why? The "why" is the part people skip.

The experience
Strand(s) — pick one or two
Learning outcome(s) — one or two, no more
Justify it — not that it grows the outcome, but how

The specific thing you will do that produces the outcome, and what will exist afterwards to show it. Cover the name of your activity: if any other activity could slot into your sentence, you have written a label, not a justification.

Brainstorm the evidence

What could you capture? Add anything — then tag each piece presence or growth. Aim for real growth in the pile, not just proof you turned up.

Organize it over time

Sam wrote two reflections for every checkpoint. They are the same length — around 95 to 120 words each — and only one of them earns the outcome. Commit to an answer before you reveal: length is not the variable.

This is the part students miss: an experience isn't one entry. Evidence piles up across weeks, and you reflect each time — not once at the end. Each reflection needs 100 words before you can move on — not to make you write more, but because under 100 words you will almost always describe what happened instead of what changed.

↑ Four entries, logged as you go — not four boxes filled the night before. The strong ones all do the same three things: name the outcome, show what changed and how you know, then say what you are changing next. That is the thread the final reflection pulls together.

The final reflection

The culmination pulls the whole thread together. Compare a weak one with a strong one — then write yours.

✗ Weak — could be about anything

✓ Strong — references the arc

    Answer these, in order: 1. What did you set out to grow?   2. What does your evidence show?   3. How did your thinking change from your first reflection to your last?   4. What next?
