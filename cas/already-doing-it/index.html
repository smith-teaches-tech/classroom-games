02 / NAME THE RULERS

Now we can say what each one is

You sorted using your own instinct. Here are the actual definitions — notice how well your instinct already matched them.

Strand 01

Creativity

Exploring and extending ideas leading to an original or interpretive product or performance. Making, designing, performing — and getting better at it.

Strand 02

Activity

Physical exertion contributing to a healthy lifestyle. Sport, training, dance, hiking — anything that moves your body with intent.

Strand 03

Service

Collaborative and reciprocal engagement with the community in response to an authentic need. You help; you also learn. Never paid, never one-directional charity.

The three questions that decide if it counts

  1. Is it voluntary? Paid work and required chores are out. CAS is something you choose.
  2. Is there real growth or challenge? If it costs you nothing and teaches you nothing, it's not CAS — it's just Tuesday.
  3. Will you reflect on it? CAS isn't the doing alone. It's the doing plus noticing what it did to you. (More on that next class.)
03 / THE SEVEN

What you're meant to get out of it

Over the next 18 months you have to show seven learning outcomes — not seven boxes to tick, but seven things CAS should grow in you. Here they are in plain English. Click one; think of a time you've already hit it.

1Know your strengths — and your growth areas.strengths

Official: Identify own strengths and develop areas for growth. You can name what you're good at and pick something you genuinely want to get better at.

2Take on a challenge and build a new skill.challenge

Official: Demonstrate that challenges have been undertaken, developing new skills in the process. Not "I did the thing I already do well" — something that was actually hard.

3Start and plan something yourself.initiate

Official: Demonstrate how to initiate and plan a CAS experience. You made it happen — you weren't just handed a slot on a sign-up sheet.

4Stick with it after the new wears off.commitment

Official: Show commitment to and perseverance in CAS experiences. You kept showing up when it stopped being novel.

5Work with others — and see why it's worth it.collaboration

Official: Demonstrate the skills and recognize the benefits of working collaboratively. Real teamwork, including the friction, and what it got you that solo wouldn't.

6Engage with something bigger than your backyard.global

Official: Demonstrate engagement with issues of global significance. A problem that reaches past you and your school — connected, even locally, to a wider issue.

7Notice the right-and-wrong of your choices.ethics

Official: Recognize and consider the ethics of choices and actions. You thought about who's affected and whether it was yours to do — before, during, and after.

04 / MYTH-BUSTERS

Four things people get wrong about CAS

Flip each one.

Myth

"CAS is 150 hours you have to log."

Actually

There's no magic hour count anymore. It's about the seven outcomes and steady involvement across your two years — quality and reflection, not a stopwatch.

Myth

"My paid job counts as service."

Actually

The moment money changes hands, it's a job, not service. The skills can be real — but CAS is what you choose to give, not what you're paid to do.

Myth

"I did it once, so I can tick it off."

Actually

One-and-done rarely reaches an outcome. What earns the outcome is the reflection — what you noticed, what changed, what you'd do differently.

Myth

"It has to be huge and impressive."

Actually

It has to be yours, real, and a genuine stretch. A small thing you truly took on beats a grand thing you just attended.
