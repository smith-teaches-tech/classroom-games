Part 3

When a secret gets out

Your publishable key is meant to be public. Your RLS policies protect your data, not the key. A secret is different. A secret works like a password for your app. Once someone else has it, it proves nothing, and you can't take it back. You can only cancel it and make a new one.

What if you put your GitHub Client Secret in config.js?

1 · Anyone can read it

config.js is public twice

It's in your public GitHub repo, and anyone can open it on your live site by adding /config.js to the address. No hacking needed. Just looking.

2 · Robots find it fast

Nobody has to be looking for you

Programs scan new public code on GitHub all day, hunting for keys and secrets. Security researchers who leaked test keys on purpose have watched them get found in under a minute.

3 · They become your app

GitHub can't tell them apart from you

With your secret, someone can talk to GitHub as Mood Tracker. Add a stolen ticket and they get your users' GitHub details. That's the second "What if" in the diagram above. Your users trusted your app's name.

4 · Deleting it doesn't fix it

Your repo remembers

Removing the line from config.js and pushing again isn't enough. Every old commit is still in your repo's history, secret included. GitHub's own advice is clear: the fix is to cancel the secret, not just delete the code.

Red flags: stop if you see one

  • Your AI asks for the secret, or writes code with a line like clientSecret: or secret: in it. Say no. Your app never needs it, because Supabase already has it.
  • You're about to paste the secret anywhere except Supabase: your AI chat, a Google Doc, a message to a friend, a note "for later".
  • You're taking a screenshot of the GitHub page while the secret is showing.
  • You search your files for the word "secret" and find anything. Do this check before every push.

If it already leaked

Don't panic and don't hide it. Tell your teacher, then cancel it right away. It takes two minutes, it's free, and the old secret stops working the moment you delete it. Steps are on the GitHub setup page.
