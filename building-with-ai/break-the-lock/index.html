Break the Lock

Building with AI · Admins, moderators and Row Level Security

The RLS rules in Supabase are the lock. If a rule lets someone change a row, they can change every cell in that row. That's why moderators and admins get their own tables, and why hiding a button never protects anything.

1The wrong way: one table for everyone

This looks like the easy way. Every user is in profiles, and a role column says who is a user, a moderator or an admin. Your app also needs this rule, so people can change their username: "Users can update their own row."

The green row is Sam's own row. The rule lets him change it. RLS can't pick out single columns, so he can change every cell in it, including role.

2The right way: new tables for moderators and admins

Take the role out of profiles. Now Sam's own row only has his username in it, and that's all he can change. Moderators and admins are listed in their own tables, and those tables have their own rules.

Same trap, different column. If you put a hidden column in posts, a moderator who is allowed to hide posts can also rewrite them. That's why hiding a post means adding a row to its own hidden_posts table.

3Your app is many tables, and rules look at other tables

RLS isn't one lock on one table. Every table has its own rules. A rule on one table can look inside another table to answer its question. "Can Mo delete this post?" is answered by looking in moderators. Click any rule to see the check run.

One catch. A rule can only look in another table if that table lets it read. That's why signed-in users can read moderators and admins, but only admins can add moderators, and nobody can add admins from the app. If your admin checks suddenly fail for everyone, check the read rule on those tables first.

4The button is not the lock

Ava is the admin, so her app shows an admin panel. Sam's app doesn't. But Sam can open the console and type the same request. Both requests arrive at the same place: the rule on the moderators table.

5Test your own app with fake accounts

Now do this for real. You need one account for each kind of person, so you can sign in as each one and try to break your app.

  1. Make four test accounts in your app: an admin, a moderator and two normal users. If Confirm email is turned off in your Supabase sign-in settings, made-up emails like sam.test@example.com will work.
  2. In the Supabase dashboard, add your admin account to the admins table by hand. The dashboard skips RLS, so it's the only safe place to make an admin.
  3. Sign in as your admin. Use your admin panel to make the moderator account a moderator.
  4. Sign in as each account and try everything below. Use the console for anything your app has no button for. Ask Gemini to write the console line for your app.
