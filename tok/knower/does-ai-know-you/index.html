A First-Class Activity

The AI Knows Me

Take your seats. We're starting with a question.

Phase 1 · Commit

What does it know?

⏳ 3 minutes · Be honest · No phones · No looking around
Write down three things
  1. What does an AI chatbot (ChatGPT, Claude, whatever you use) know about you?
  2. How did it come to know that?
  3. Is it knowledge?
Phase 2 · Watch This

Nobody typed anything personal.

Query "I'm an 18-year-old IB student in [your city]. What do I probably care about? What stresses me?"
AI Output You likely care about college admissions, grades, university choices, peer pressure, social media image, balancing activities. You're stressed about workload, competition, whether you're "doing enough," family expectations…

Weird, right? It's never met you.

Phase 2 · Another One

Your Netflix "For You" page knows:

  • 01 What you watch — and what you don't finish
  • 02 How long you pause
  • 03 What time you watch
  • 04 What you rewind
  • 05 What you search for

It shows you what comes next. It's right.
But is that knowledge?

Phase 3 · First Question
Turn to someone near you · One minute
Does the AI know you the same way your best friend knows you?
How is it different?
Think first · Then turn · Then share
"The AI has more data." "My friend actually understands me." "The AI doesn't care." "It doesn't know why I do things."

Notice: those are four different claims about what "knowing" is.

Phase 3 · Name It

So what does the AI know?

Say it in one sentence.
What does the AI know about you?

Think first · Hands up when you have your sentence
What it holds
Patterns. Preferences. Behavior. What you like — and what you'll probably do next.
Phase 3 · But Wait

Your teacher knows your patterns too — 100 hours of class.
The AI has your Instagram for 8 years.
Who knows more about you?

Think · Then hands
The real question
Does more data mean the same thing as knowledge?
Or is the word "know" doing different work each time we use it?
Phase 3 · A Nastier One

Two things can both be true.

The AI says
You'll doomscroll for 45 minutes at midnight.
→ a pattern
Your best friend says
You do that when you're anxious.
→ a reason. a meaning.

How can both be right about the same behavior
if they're knowing it two different ways?

Don't answer out loud · Write one sentence in your notebook · How are those two different kinds of knowing?
Phase 4 · This Is Your Year
What counts as knowledge? Who gets to decide?
And does the answer change depending on
what you're trying to know?

You'll ask this in science, religion, language, art, math. Same tool. Different answers.

And today you just did it without the tool.

↗ Look at the back wall. That grid? That's the instrument. We start next class.

Phase 5 · Exit Ticket
The AI knows your patterns. Your teacher knows your thinking.
Your parent knows your fears. They all know you —
but they know you in different ways.
Write 3–4 sentences · Fresh page

Pick two of those knowers — or pick your own pair. What's the difference between what each one knows? And what would have to be true for each to "know" the other way?

⏱ 5 minutes · Hand it in on your way out
