AP Language · Day Two · 45 Minutes
Move the Situation
First · 5 minutes · whole class, together

Where the Room Stands

Hands up, five apologies, one at a time. Your teacher runs this from the front and taps the numbers in. We want to see which apologies split the room — a split means the ranking was never as obvious as it felt when you did it.

The row highlighted in yellow is the apology this class disagrees about most. Write it down — you will want it later.

After the poll, you are on your own. Everything from here you work through with your partner, at your own pace. Read carefully, argue properly, and do not press a reveal button until you have both committed to an answer out loud.

Second · 6 minutes · with your partner

Warm-Up: Change One Thing

Kanye West's blog post from 2009, word for word as he wrote it. Nothing in the text changes. The only thing that changes is who is saying it. Work through both steps before you reveal anything.

Third · about 24 minutes · with your partner

Four Situations, Four Apologies

Each card keeps one apology exactly as its speaker wrote it and changes one element of the rhetorical situation. Work through them in any order. Do at least two properly — properly beats quickly, and rushing all four is worth less than arguing your way through two.

Cards worked through

How to work a card. Read what changed. Answer Step One out loud with your partner and write your answer down — then reveal ours and see whether you agree. Then answer Step Two by actually writing the new line, not describing it, and reveal ours only after your line exists on paper.

Our reading is not the answer key. If you disagree with it, say why in your notebook. Each card has a box at the bottom for exactly that.

✎ In your notebook, for each card you work
  1. The apology, and which element of the situation changed.
  2. The quoted words that stop working, and one sentence on why they stop working.
  3. Your replacement line, written out in full.
  4. If you disagreed with our reading anywhere, one sentence saying where and why.
Fourth · 5 minutes

The Rhetorical Situation Is Not Static

Four apologies. Not one word of any original text was changed.

What changed each time was a single element of the situation the apology was made in:

SpeakerAudienceVenueTiming

And every time, choices that had been working stopped working, or choices that had been failing suddenly held.

So the rhetorical situation is not background you describe once and then set aside before the real analysis starts. It is the thing that decides whether a choice works. One sentence can be the strongest line in an apology and the weakest line in that same apology, depending on who says it, to whom, where, and when.

Which is why an apology cannot be judged on its own. There is no such thing as a well-written apology in general. There is only an apology that fits the person who has to give it, the people who have to accept it, and the moment it arrives in.

Rhetoric is the use of the means of persuasion available to you. You have now watched what counts as available change four times in twenty minutes.

✎ In your notebook
  1. Finish this sentence in your own words: “A choice is available to a speaker when…”
  2. One sentence on what changed in your thinking today. If nothing changed, write the strongest objection you and your partner could not answer.
Last · 5 minutes

Thursday: You Fix One

You have just written replacement lines for other people's apologies under situations we invented. On Thursday you do it properly, for the real situation, and then you explain your own choices the way you have been explaining theirs.

Thursday's task. Choose one apology you put in Failed or Mixed. Rewrite it in that person's voice. Then analyse two or three of your own choices using Choice, Unpacking, and Effect on the Audience.

✎ Before you leave — write these two things
  1. Which apology you are rewriting. It must be one you tiered Failed or Mixed.
  2. The one specific failure you are repairing. Not “make it better.” Quote the phrase that fails and name the dodge. For example: “I was disrespectful to the officer” — the Downgrade. Reese never names the actual offence.

Arrive Thursday with those two lines already written and you spend the whole period writing instead of deciding.

AP Language · Mr. Smith · Substitute Notes

Move the Situation — 45 minutes

You run one poll at the front for five minutes. After that the website runs itself and students work in pairs at their own screens. Nothing to teach, nothing to collect — it all goes in notebooks.

Last class they sorted nine celebrity apologies into Effective, Mixed and Failed. Today they discover that those rankings change when the situation around the apology changes, and they practise writing a replacement line.

The only part you run

Open the first tab on the projector. It lists five apologies. For each one, ask for hands — who put it in Effective, who in Mixed, who in Failed — and tap the numbers into the buttons on screen. About a minute each. One row will turn yellow, which simply means the class was most divided about that apology. Point it out and move on; you do not need to discuss it.

Then tell them to work through the rest of the tabs with their partner at their own pace, and that they have roughly thirty minutes before the last tab.

What they are doing after that

A short warm-up about Kanye West's apology, then four cards. Each card takes one apology, keeps the words exactly as written, and changes one thing about the situation — who is speaking, who is listening, where it was posted, or when it was said. Students work out which words stop working, then write a replacement line themselves. They are told to do at least two cards properly rather than rushing all four.

The last two tabs are a short conclusion and Thursday's assignment. Make sure everybody reaches the final tab with about five minutes left, because the two lines they write there are what Mr. Smith needs on Thursday.

If they ask you something

If a pair asks whether they have the right answer, tell them there is no single right answer and that each step has a reveal button giving our reasoning for them to argue with. If a pair says the replacement line is too hard to write, tell them one sentence is enough. If a pair finishes all four cards early, have them write a second replacement line for a different part of the same apology.

Their work saves automatically on the computer they are using. If a screen is carrying another class's work, the Start Fresh button at the top right clears it.
