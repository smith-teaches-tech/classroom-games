AP Language & Composition · The Rhetorical Situation
Sorry Not Sorry
Stage One · 12 minutes

The Swap

Two apologies. Each one appears twice. In both cases we changed only the style — not a single fact, not a single claim, not one thing the speaker is actually saying. Read carefully and answer before you scroll.

Pair One · Kanye West, 2009

At the 2009 MTV Video Music Awards, Taylor Swift won Best Female Video. During her acceptance speech Kanye West took the microphone and said Beyoncé deserved it more. He posted this apology on his blog the next day.
Version A · What he actually wrote

I'm sooooo sorry to Taylor Swift and her fans and her mom. I spoke to her mother right after and she said the same thing my mother would've said. She is very talented! I like the lyrics about being a cheerleader and she's in the bleachers! I'm in the wrong for going on stage and taking away from her moment!

Beyoncé's video was the best of this decade!!!! I'm sorry to my fans if I let you guys down!!!! I'm sorry to my friends at MTV. I will apologize to Taylor 2mrw. Welcome to the real world!!!! Everybody wanna booooo me but I'm a fan of real pop culture!!! No disrespect but we watchin' the show at the crib right now cause ... well you know!!!! I'm still happy for Taylor!!!! Boooyaaawwww!!!! You are very very talented!!! I gave my awards to Outkast when they deserved it over me ... That's what it is!!!!!!! I'm not crazy y'all, i'm just real. Sorry for that!!! I really feel bad for Taylor and I'm sincerely sorry!!! Much respect!!!!!

Version B · Same content, cleaned up

I would like to offer my sincere apologies to Taylor Swift, her family, and her supporters. I spoke with her mother shortly after the incident, and she expressed what my own mother would have said. Taylor is a talented artist, and I was wrong to take the stage and diminish her moment.

That said, I maintain that Beyoncé's video was the finest of the decade. I also apologize to my fans, should I have disappointed them, and to my colleagues at MTV. I intend to speak with Taylor personally tomorrow. I have given awards to other artists when they deserved them over me. I am not unreasonable; I am simply candid. I regret what happened, and I remain happy for Taylor.

Answer with your partner
Version B fixes every spelling, grammar and punctuation problem in Version A. Is it a better apology? Explain what did or did not change.
Write something before you reveal. That is the whole point.
A stronger answer

No — and it may be worse. Nothing that was wrong with this apology was located in the language.

  • He apologizes and re-argues the verdict in the same breath. I'm in the wrong for going on stage sits a few words away from Beyoncé's video was the best of this decade. He is still making his case.
  • He defers the apology that matters: I will apologize to Taylor 2mrw. The one person he wronged is the one audience he has not spoken to yet.
  • Welcome to the real world is aimed at his critics, not at Taylor. So is Everybody wanna booooo me.
  • He apologizes to his fans for letting them down, which quietly makes him the person who suffered a loss.

Now notice what the cleanup did. In Version A the exclamation points at least read as a man typing fast, genuinely agitated, at one in the morning. In Version B he has clearly had time to sit and compose — and he still decided to defend himself. The polish makes the self-defense look considered instead of impulsive. The grammar improved and the apology got worse.

Pair Two · Reese Witherspoon, 2013

Reese Witherspoon was arrested in Atlanta in 2013 after her husband was pulled over for drunk driving. She was reported to have asked the arresting officer, “Do you know my name?” She released a written statement and later apologized on television.
Version A · What she actually said

I clearly had one drink too many and I am deeply embarrassed about the things I said. It was definitely a scary situation and I was frightened for my husband, but that is no excuse. I was disrespectful to the officer who was just doing his job. I have nothing but respect for the police and I'm very sorry for my behavior.

It's one of those nights, you know, we went out to dinner in Atlanta and we had one too many glasses of wine and we thought we were fine to drive and we absolutely were not. And it's just completely unacceptable and we are so sorry and embarrassed. And we know better. And we shouldn't have done that.

Version B · Same content, different voice

ok so I had like ONE too many drinks!!!! sooooo embarrassed about what I said!!! it was scary!!! I was freaking out about my husband!!! but no excuses!!!!

I was rude to that officer who was just doing his job!!!! I love cops!!!! sorry!!!! It's one of those nights y'all!!! we had dinner in Atlanta, had a few glasses of wine, thought we were fine to drive and we were NOT!!!! totally unacceptable!!!! we know better!!!! my bad!!!!!

Answer with your partner
Version B keeps every single thing Reese admits to. Only the voice changed. Is it a worse apology? Why should that be, if the content is identical?
Write something before you reveal.
A stronger answer

Yes — badly. Nothing she admits to has changed. But a film actress doing damage control after a drunk driving arrest, speaking to a mainstream audience that includes people who have lost family members to drunk drivers, cannot be breezy about it. Being casual reads as not understanding how serious this was, and not understanding how serious it was is exactly the thing she needs to disprove.

Here is the part worth sitting with. That style was available to Kanye. A musician posting on his own blog in 2009, to fans who follow him precisely because he does not sound like a press release — that voice fits him, his platform, and his audience. The same voice is not available to Reese, in a written statement to the general public, about a criminal offense.

Neither version is better writing. They are different sets of options, belonging to different people in different situations.

So here is the definition you are working with for the rest of this unit.

Rhetoric is the art of using the means of persuasion that are actually available to you.

Available is the word doing the work. What is available to a speaker depends on who they are, who is listening, what they did, and where they are speaking. Kanye cannot sound like a corporate statement without sounding fake. A CEO cannot say my bad without sounding like he is not taking it seriously.

You already know this. You do not ask your mother for something the same way you ask your father, and you did not pick one of those approaches because it was better writing. You picked it because you knew your audience. That instinct is the thing we are about to make formal.

✎ In your notebook — heading: THE SWAP TEST
  1. One sentence: why did fixing Kanye's grammar fail to fix his apology?
  2. One sentence: why did Reese's apology break when only the style changed?
  3. Copy the definition: Rhetoric is the art of using the means of persuasion that are actually available to you.
Stage Two · 8 minutes

What Is an Apology Actually For?

In a few minutes you are going to rank nine public apologies from most to least effective. You cannot judge whether something is effective until you know what it was trying to do. So decide that first, before you read a single one.

With your partner · four minutes
What is a public apology trying to accomplish? Be specific. “To say sorry” is not an answer — that describes the words, not the goal. What does the speaker need to be true when it is over?
Commit to an answer first. Then compare.
A stronger answer

A public apology tries to restore the speaker's standing with an audience that has lost trust in them.

It succeeds when that audience believes two things:

  • This person understands what they actually did. Not a softened version of it. The thing itself.
  • This person is not going to do it again.

Everything else is a means to those two ends. Notice what is not on that list: sounding sincere, sounding professional, sounding sorry, sounding polished. Those are impressions, and impressions are easy to fake. The question is whether a specific group of people would actually forgive this specific person.

Which means every judgment you make from here has to name an audience. Forgiven by whom?

✎ In your notebook — heading: PURPOSE
  1. Your definition, as you wrote it.
  2. The revised definition, and the two things the audience has to believe.
Stage Three · 10 minutes

Moves and Dodges

You know the goal. Now: what choices can a speaker make to get there? And what does it look like when a speaker performs the choice without actually making it?

With your partner · four minutes
List as many specific strategies as you can that would make an audience believe a speaker understands what they did and will not do it again. Aim for at least four.
Four strategies, then reveal.
Five moves, and the dodge that imitates each one
1 · Name It
Say what you did, specifically, using the words your audience would use for it.
The Downgrade
Apologize for a smaller, vaguer offense than the one you actually committed. The audience knows what you did. You name something adjacent to it and hope that counts.
2 · Own It
Grammatically and morally. Somebody did this, and the sentence should contain them.
The Agentless Dodge
Build a sentence with nobody in it. Things happened. Pain was caused. Events occurred. Watch for passive voice and for verbs that turn a decision into weather.
The Intent Defense
“I never meant it that way.” This asks to be judged on your intentions instead of on what you did to somebody.
3 · Center Them
The injured party is the subject. Not you, and not what this has cost you.
The Redemption Pivot
Turn the apology into the story of your personal growth. What I've learned. How I've changed. How hard this year has been for me.
Speaking for the Victim
Announce what the injured person wants — usually that they want it to be over. You are now deciding when their story ends.
4 · No Excuses
Do not re-argue the case inside the apology. An apology and a defense cannot occupy the same paragraph.
The Re-litigation
Apologize, then explain why you were sort of right, or why the other person provoked it, or why everyone else does it too.
5 · Repair It
Offer something concrete, costly and checkable. Money, resignation, cancelled work, a date by which something will be done. Feelings are free; repair is not.
No dodge exists for this one
You cannot fake a concrete commitment. Its absence is the tell. When an apology contains no repair, ask what the speaker is actually willing to lose.

And two conditions that govern all five:

  • Right audience, right venue. Who needs to hear this, and is that who is actually being addressed? Where was it published, and who reads that?
  • Register that fits the speaker. Stage One. The voice has to be one this person can plausibly use.

Now the warning, and this is the part that separates a real analysis from a checklist.

This is not a scoring rubric. Two of the nine apologies you are about to read run the identical Redemption Pivot — nearly the same sentence — and only one of them survives it. The other one is sunk by it.

A move is not good or bad on its own. It works or fails depending on who is making it, what they did, who is listening, and what surrounds it in the text. Finding the dodge is the beginning of your analysis. Explaining why it lands or fails here, for this speaker, with this audience is the analysis itself.

✎ In your notebook — heading: MOVES AND DODGES
  1. The five moves, in your own words. One line each.
  2. The six dodges, by name, with a one-line definition of each. You will be citing these by name for the rest of the period, so get them down properly.
Stage Four · 12 minutes

How to Actually Read One

Watch the tools get used. This is Reese Witherspoon's apology again — the full version this time. Most people read it as fine. It is not fine, and the reasons are all at the level of the phrase.

Arrested in Atlanta, April 2013, after her husband was pulled over for driving under the influence. She was reported to have asked the arresting officer: “Do you know my name?” — an attempt to use her celebrity to avoid arrest. That detail was in every story about the incident. Her audience already knew it when she spoke.
The statement, in full

I clearly had one drink too many and I am deeply embarrassed about the things I said. It was definitely a scary situation and I was frightened for my husband, but that is no excuse. I was disrespectful to the officer who was just doing his job. I have nothing but respect for the police and I'm very sorry for my behavior.

It's one of those nights, you know, we went out to dinner in Atlanta and we had one too many glasses of wine and we thought we were fine to drive and we absolutely were not. And it's just completely unacceptable and we are so sorry and embarrassed. And we know better. And we shouldn't have done that.

I have no idea what I was saying that night. I saw him arresting my husband and I literally panicked. And I said all kinds of crazy things. I told him I was pregnant — I'm not pregnant! I said crazy things. And if you only hear me laughing it's because I have no idea what I was talking about. And I'm so sorry. I was so disrespectful to him and I have police officers in my family. I work with police officers every day. I know better and it's just unacceptable.

Four phrases. For each one, work out with your partner what the phrase is doing, then choose. Every option below is something a reasonable person has said about this apology — more than one of them is even true. Only one names what the phrase accomplishes for her. If you pick wrong you will get told why, and you go again.

Now read the statement one more time with those four in view, and ask the only question that matters: after hearing this, would her audience believe she understands what she actually did?

Notice what you did not have to say to get here. Nothing about her grammar. Nothing about whether she sounds sincere. You quoted phrases and explained what each one accomplishes. That is the whole method, and it is what you owe every apology in the next stage.

✎ In your notebook — heading: CLOSE READ · WITHERSPOON
  1. All four phrases, quoted exactly.
  2. Beside each: what it is doing, and the name of the dodge if it is one.
  3. Any option you chose that turned out to be wrong — write it down with one line on why it was wrong. Those are the four mistakes everybody makes, and naming yours is worth more than getting it right first try.
  4. One sentence answering the question in the box above.
Stage Five · Reference

The Nine Apologies

If you read and annotated these for homework, use this tab to find the phrases you marked. If you did not, read them now — and read for quotable phrases, not for a general impression. You will need to cite exact language in the next stage.

As you read, mark: the sentence where the speaker names what they did (or the place where that sentence should be and isn't); any sentence with no person in it; anything the speaker offers to actually give up; and the moment, if it comes, when the apology turns into a story about the speaker.

Stage Six · 24 minutes

Tier and Defend

Sort all nine into three tiers, and justify every placement with a quoted phrase and a named move or dodge. You are not rating how sorry the person seems. You are deciding whether that speaker's audience would forgive them, and pointing at the language that decides it.

Effective — this audience would likely forgive this speaker, and you can point at why.
Mixed — real strengths and real failures. You could argue it either way, and you should be able to argue both.
Failed — this audience would not forgive, and the text is the reason.

Reasons that do not count. Bad grammar. Too short. Too long. Sounds fake. Doesn't sound sincere. They're a bad person. I don't like them.

The test: if your reason would still be true had the speaker done something completely different, it is not a reason about this apology. “It's vague” is not enough either — vague about what, and what is the vagueness hiding?

Exit Slip · last 10 minutes

The Available Means

This one you do on your own — not with your partner. It is posted on Google Classroom and you submit it there. Use your notebook and this site; you are supposed to look things up.

Aristotle defined rhetoric as the ability to see, in any given case, the available means of persuasion.

Answer all three

  1. What does “available” mean in that definition? What decides which means are available to a particular speaker? Do not just define the word — explain what limits the options.
  2. One apology that used its available means well. Quote a phrase. Explain why that choice was available to that speaker, and what it accomplished with that audience.
  3. One apology that did not. Quote a phrase. Then explain either what was available to that speaker that they failed to use, or what they reached for that was never available to them in the first place.

Three rules.

You must quote. No quote, no credit.

Nothing about grammar, spelling, length, or whether somebody “sounds sincere.”

Name the audience every time. Forgiven by whom?

Six to eight sentences is enough. Write it as a paragraph, not as three answers to three questions.

AP Language · Mr. Smith · Substitute Notes

Sorry Not Sorry — 80 minutes

You do not need to know anything about rhetoric to run this. The site does the teaching. Your job is pairs, timing, and making sure they are writing.

Setup

  • Students work in pairs, one screen per pair. Pairs matter — the arguing is the assignment.
  • Everything goes in notebooks. Nothing is collected, nothing is submitted, nothing needs to be printed or emailed. Mr. Smith checks notebooks next class.
  • Each stage ends with a boxed instruction telling them exactly what to write down. Point at it if they skip it.
  • If a screen gets reset or a pair takes over a machine from another class, the Start Fresh button is top right.

Timing

0–5Settle, pairs, screens open. Tell them: everything gets written in notebooks.
5–17Stage 1 · The Swap. Two apologies, each shown twice. They must type an answer before the site reveals ours. Do not let them skip to the reveal.
17–25Stage 2 · Purpose. Four minutes writing, then reveal, then notebook.
25–35Stage 3 · Moves and Dodges. Four minutes writing, then reveal. They must copy the five moves and six dodges by name. They need them for the rest of the period.
35–47Stage 4 · Close Read. Four phrases, each a multiple choice. Wrong answers explain why and let them try again. Getting one wrong is useful here — don't let them rush to the right letter without reading the response.
46–70Stage 6 · Tier and Defend. The main event. Stage 5 is just the nine texts for reference — they can read from it as they go.
70–80Exit slip. See below. Start this at 70 minutes even if they are not finished tiering.

If you fall behind, take the time out of Stage 5 reading, not Stage 6. Tiering is the point of the lesson.

The last ten minutes

  • At 70 minutes, stop the pair work and tell them to open the last tab of the site, called Exit Slip.
  • They work alone for this part, not in pairs.
  • They may use their notebooks and the website. That is intended — it is not a test.
  • They submit it on Google Classroom, where the same questions are posted. You do not collect anything.
  • Before they leave, just confirm each student has submitted. That is the only thing that has to be handed in today.

Things students will ask you

  • “Is this one good or bad?” — Don't answer. Say: “Reread the phrase you quoted and tell me whether that speaker's audience would forgive them.”
  • “We disagree.” — Perfect. They do not have to agree. Both partners write down their own reason. That is literally the last item in their notebook.
  • “Can we just say it's badly written?” — No. The site lists banned reasons in Stage 6. Point them at that box.
  • “Do we have to do all nine?” — Yes, all nine get a tier. The written rationale is required for every one.

Content note

These are real public apologies for real offenses, and several involve serious misconduct — a drunk driving arrest, a homophobic slur, sexual misconduct allegations, an invasion of privacy, a political controversy. Students have the context paragraphs. This is an AP course and these texts are part of the unit. If a student raises a concern, let them switch to analyzing a different one and let Mr. Smith know.

If a pair finishes early

Send them back to the two apologies they disagreed about and have them write the strongest possible case against their own placement. Or have them find a second quoted phrase for every apology in their Failed tier.
