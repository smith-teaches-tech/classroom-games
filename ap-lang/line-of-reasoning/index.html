The Volupides Case: Building an Arrest Warrant
Before you begin

What the officers found

You already know how to build one syllogism — data + warrant → claim. A warrant that will actually convince a judge needs more than that. It needs those claims arranged so each one stands on the ground the last one laid. That arrangement is the line of reasoning.

The opening paragraph — this is given to you

INCIDENT REPORT 4471-B Volupides residence · 1:47 a.m.
Request for Questioning

We arrived at the residence of Arthur and Queenie Volupides at 1:47 a.m. Arthur was at the bottom of the stairs, lying face up, and was declared dead at the scene of a wound to the head. In his hand was an unbroken glass, and he smelled strongly of alcohol. Mrs. Volupides stated that she had returned home from the country club to find her husband unresponsive at the foot of the stairs, and that he had fallen. She telephoned police ten minutes later. On the basis of the findings below, we respectfully request that Mrs. Volupides be brought in for questioning.

Notice what the warrant does not say. It does not say Queenie killed her husband. There is no weapon, no witness, and no evidence of who struck the blow — so no line of reasoning can carry us there. It asks for questioning, and every paragraph that follows has to earn exactly that much and no more.

An argument that claims more than its evidence supports doesn't sound stronger. It collapses.

Evidence available at the scene

  • 01Arthur lay face up, feet pointed toward the bottom step.
  • 02An unbroken glass was still in his hand.
  • 03No liquid had been spilled on the steps or the carpet.
  • 04The stair carpet was not ruffled or pulled loose.
  • 05The mirror and wall lamps along the staircase were undisturbed.
  • 06The fatal wound was to the back of the skull.
  • 07No bruising or scrapes on his forearms or palms.
  • 08The dining table was set for two; two steaks waited in the broiler.
  • 09The ice in the bowl beside the glasses had melted entirely.
  • 10Mrs. Volupides was still wearing her outdoor shoes.
  • 11She waited ten minutes to call police — until her friends arrived.
  • 12She said Arthur "fell down the stairs," though she says she found him already at the bottom.

Three rules the puzzle will teach you.

1. Nothing before its foundation. If a claim uses an earlier claim as its data, it cannot come first.
2. Follow the hooks. A paragraph that opens by naming an earlier finding cannot be that earlier finding.
3. Chronology is not logic. What happened first that night is rarely what belongs first in the argument.

How to get through this. Five gates, in order. Each one stays locked until you've cleared the one before it, and the finished warrant stays sealed until you've cleared them all.

Gate 1 of 4

Order the paragraphs

Five body paragraphs, scrambled. Each one is already written correctly on the inside — your job is the order. Drag by the handle, or use the arrows.

How to crack it Read only the first sentence of each paragraph. Four of the five begin by naming something that has already been proven — "If Arthur did not fall…", "If another person caused that injury…". A paragraph that leans on an earlier finding cannot come before it. One paragraph leans on nothing at all. That one goes first, and the rest fall into place behind it.
Fixed · Paragraph 1

We arrived at the residence of Arthur and Queenie Volupides at 1:47 a.m. … On the basis of the findings below, we respectfully request that Mrs. Volupides be brought in for questioning.
