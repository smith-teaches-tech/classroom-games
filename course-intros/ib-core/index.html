Year 1 · meet the three, one at a time
EE

Extended Essay

A 4,000-word research paper on a question your child chooses, mentored by a supervisor. Their first real taste of university-level independent research.

It teaches how to ask a good question, gather and weigh evidence, and sustain a line of reasoning.

In Year 1 we choose the subject and question, and begin research.
Presenter · Year 1
Reassure: they don’t do this alone or all at once. Supervisor + scaffolded steps across the year.

CAS

Creativity · Activity · Service

Learning that happens outside the classroom: something creative, something physical, and something that serves others.

Students plan experiences, reflect on them, and collect evidence in a portfolio — it’s about growth, not hours logged.

In Year 1 CAS gets moving — turning “what you care about” into real experiences.
Presenter · Year 1
Emphasise reflection over box-ticking. Encourage families to support experiences their child genuinely cares about.
TOK

Theory of Knowledge

The hardest and most interesting piece. TOK asks one deceptively simple question:

“How do you know that’s true?”

Not what to think, but how we come to know — and where knowledge can be trusted, doubted, or misused. Here are a couple of examples…

Presenter · Year 1
TOK isn’t more content to memorise — it’s a habit of asking for justification. The next two slides show it in action.
A knowledge claim
“There is intelligent life on other planets.”
How would you decide? What would count as evidence — and is the absence of it evidence of anything? TOK examines how we reason about what we can’t yet observe.
Presenter · Year 1
The goal isn’t an answer — it’s noticing what standard of proof we demand. Let a couple of parents react.
A different kind of claim
“Climate is caused by human activity.”
Here we do have mountains of evidence. So TOK asks a different question: how does a whole community of experts build confidence, and why do people weigh the same evidence so differently?
Presenter · Year 1
Contrast with the previous claim: one is short on evidence, one is rich in it. Same TOK move — interrogate how the knowing works. Keep it neutral; the point is method, not politics.
Year 1 · a word about Turnitin & AI

The detector isn’t guessing

We didn’t take Turnitin’s word for it — we tested it ourselves, and it was accurate.

<1%
false-positive rate at our generous 25% threshold — and zero in our own 44-document test
77%
of flagged students admitted AI use when offered a no-penalty chance to come clean
0
clean-scored students who ever came forward to say they were wrongly flagged
So if your child is ever flagged, it is very rarely a mistake. Our response is always a conversation first — and honesty is the best opening move.

Presenter · short & direct
The point isn’t to threaten — it’s to pre-empt the reflex of “the tool must be wrong.” Our own data says it almost never is. Land the 77% and the zero, then move to the process.
Year 1 · academic integrity & AI

Our approach to AI and integrity is a fair, consistent process. Any flag — missing citations, a high AI score, or sources that don’t check out — leads first to a conversation. If a student can explain their work and produce their sources, it’s resolved.

Presenter · Year 1
Frame as fairness, not suspicion. Students are never penalised on a score alone — they always get to explain and show their process.
