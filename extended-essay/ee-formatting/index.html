Professor Plagiarism

"Half MLA, half APA, and one source that never made it to the bibliography. Delicious."

Threat: academic misconduct + Criterion A consistency
⚡ Hero Power: One style, chosen once, applied everywhere. The IB does not care which style — it cares that it is complete and consistent, and that every in-text citation lands in the bibliography and vice versa.

Wave 1 · Where does the full stop go?

Wave 2 · Italics or quotation marks?

Big containers go in italics. Things inside containers go in "quotation marks". Sort them.

Italics — whole works

"Quotes" — parts of works

Wave 3 · Every citation needs a home

Drop each in-text citation onto the bibliography entry it points to.

Brown, Sarah. "Technology in Education." Academic Journal, vol. 12, 2023, pp. 80–95.

Johnson, Mark. Modern Research Methods. Academic Press, 2022.

Smith, Jane. "Climate Policy Analysis." Environmental Studies Quarterly, vol. 8, no. 3, 2023, pp. 40–58.

Wave 4 · The hanging indent

⚡ Hero Power: First line at the margin, every line after it indented 0.5". It makes surnames scannable — which is the whole point of an alphabetised bibliography.
✓ Hanging indent
Smith, Jane. "Climate Policy Analysis." Environmental Studies Quarterly, vol. 8, no. 3, 2023, pp. 40–58.
✕ No indent
Smith, Jane. "Climate Policy Analysis." Environmental Studies Quarterly, vol. 8, no. 3, 2023, pp. 40–58.

Google Docs: select the entries → Format → Align & indent → Indentation options → Special indent → Hanging → 0.5" → Apply. Word: select → Ctrl/⌘ + T. Never fake it with the space bar or Enter — it collapses the moment the text reflows.

Quick check: your bibliography lists a source you read early on, found useful for background, but never cited in the final essay. What do you do?

📋 Your pre-submission sweep — print this

  1. Title page: title, research question, subject (and category), word count, blank line for your personal code. No name, no school, no supervisor.
  2. Personal code: arrives from your coordinator around late December — fill the blank line the day you get it.
  3. Contents page: every heading exists in the essay, worded identically, with page numbers that are true today. No abstract.
  4. References: one style throughout; every in-text citation appears in the bibliography and vice versa; hanging indents; punctuation inside the sentence.
  5. Visuals: numbered, titled, units labelled, source given, and referred to and interpreted in the text.
  6. Word count: introduction + body + conclusion + quotations + explanatory footnotes ≤ 4,000. References, contents page, tables, charts, equations, bibliography and appendices sit outside it.
  7. Layout: 12pt readable font, double spacing, 2.54 cm margins, page numbers, consistent headings.
  8. Last: re-check the word count and the contents page after your final edit, not before.
