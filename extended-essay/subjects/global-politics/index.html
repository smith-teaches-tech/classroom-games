What makes a Global Politics EE different (guide p. 55–56, 59)

Global politics is in the individuals and societies group, so your essay explores a question about human experience and behaviour. The guide's two global politics examples follow one pattern. First, a literature review sets out key political concepts, such as power, conflict or the right to privacy. Next comes analysis of one or more real cases. Last, an evaluation links the facts of each case with the theories.

In global politics, relevant academic secondary sources back up your argument. You may also add primary data collected with suitable methods, but you don't have to. Your content alone won't make your essay a global politics essay. Global politics concepts, methods and theories are what let you build a well-constructed argument.

Here is how a broad interest like sport becomes a global politics essay: you explore global discourses (the ways people talk and write) about women in sports, and how those discourses shape political decisions about gender rights. The guide also names migration and governance in a digital age as real-world issues for the group.

Primary and secondary sources (guide p. 56, 59)

Primary

Primary data is optional in global politics. Academic secondary sources carry your argument, and you may add primary data collected with suitable methods. For example, the guide's Snapchat example analyses the app's own privacy policy and the personal data the app needs.

Secondary

Relevant academic secondary sources back up your argument, and every extended essay must include secondary research (a literature review). Both global politics examples in the guide open with a literature review of the key concepts.

How researchers in Global Politics answer questions (guide p. 55–56, 59)

  • Literature review of key political concepts, such as power, conflict, terrorism, leadership, feminism in politics and soft power. Your literature review builds the framework for your analysis.
  • Comparing cases in two countries, as in the example comparing responses to terrorist attacks in New Zealand and France.
  • Analysing one case through the documents the case produces, as in the example analysing Snapchat's data requirements and privacy policy.
  • Discourse analysis: the guide's sport example explores global discourses about women in sports.
  • Other research strategies for all individuals and societies subjects: case studies, critical analysis of a theory or concept, and in-depth analysis of a primary source.

Choosing a topic (guide p. 55–56, 59)

  • Your topic can come from your own life, from a current debate, from a topic in one of your favourite subjects, or from a topic related to a favourite subject but not covered in it.
  • Big real-world issues, such as migration, governance in a digital age or the environmental and climate crisis, can work. Turn the issue into a focused question and explore it from a global politics point of view.
  • Your topic must be considered in terms of one individuals and societies subject. A broad interest such as sport could fit several subjects. The guide's global politics version explores discourses about women in sports and political decisions about gender rights.
  • Your approach decides your subject. The concepts, methods and theories of global politics are what ground your topic in global politics.
  • Both global politics research questions in the guide name the case inside the question: New Zealand and France in one, Snapchat in the European Union in the other.

Examples from the guide

Topic and research questionWhy it works
Women, power and conflict
To what extent is female leadership better at facing conflict? Responses to terrorist attacks in New Zealand and France.		The essay opens with a literature review of the concepts of power, conflict, terrorism, leadership, feminism in politics and soft power. Next, the essay analyses terrorist attacks and the responses to them in New Zealand under Prime Minister Jacinda Ardern and in France under President Emmanuel Macron. An evaluation of both cases leads to a conclusion that links facts with theories. (p. 59)
Big data, apps and human rights
To what extent is the large amount of personal data collected by mobile apps a breach of the right to privacy? The case of Snapchat in the European Union (EU).		The essay reviews the literature on big data, human rights, universalism, interdependence, indivisibility, the right to privacy and data protection. Next, the essay analyses the personal data needed to download and use Snapchat, and Snapchat's privacy policy. The facts are then linked with the key concepts to build an argument and a conclusion that answers the research question. (p. 59)

How the essay is usually built (guide p. 56, 59, 62–63)

  • The guide's global politics examples move in three stages: a literature review of key concepts, analysis of the case or cases, and an evaluation that links the facts with the theories.
  • Make sure your research question and research methods clearly fit global politics. The guide suggests reading the global politics subject guide to check that you understand the subject and its methods.
  • Keep your argument within the logic of global politics: use the subject's terms and follow its usual style and structure.
  • Label diagrams, images, graphs and maps clearly, with all required elements (such as a scale and a key or legend). Acknowledge any made by others, and refer to each one directly in the body of your essay.
  • Use headings to organise your essay where they help.

Common problems to avoid (guide p. 56, 59, 62–63)

  • Relying on content alone. Without global politics concepts, methods and theories, your essay won't be grounded in global politics.
  • Keeping facts and theories apart. Both of the guide's examples end by linking the facts of the case to the concepts from the literature review.
  • A research question or research methods that don't clearly fit global politics.
  • A one-sided discussion. A balanced discussion looks at different perspectives on your topic, and your evaluation should weigh up the strengths and limitations of your essay.
  • Using key concepts without applying them. The guide says applying key concepts in your writing is essential.
  • Maps, diagrams or graphs that are unlabelled, missing a scale or key, unacknowledged, or never referred to in the body of your essay.

Rules for Global Politics EEs (guide p. 55–56)

  • Your essay must include secondary research (a literature review).
  • Your topic must be considered in terms of global politics, even if the same topic could be approached through another individuals and societies subject.
