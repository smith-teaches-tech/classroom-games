Research methods in your subject

Every subject has its own ways of answering questions. Learn the methods your subject trusts before you write your question.

Why this step matters

Criterion A asks whether your research methods provide an effective framework for your essay. The question you write in step 10 has to be one that your subject's methods can actually answer.

Every extended essay must include secondary research — a review of the literature. Most subjects also expect primary research. What counts as primary and secondary is different in each subject.

Methods by subject

SubjectTypical approachPrimary sourcesSecondary sources
Language AClose textual analysis of your primary text(s): language, structure, literary devices and their effects.The novels, poems, plays, films (screenplay) or other texts you analyse.Literary criticism, studies of the author or genre, journal articles, literary theory. A review of existing criticism is required.
Language BAnalysis of texts originally written or spoken in the target language — as literature, as language use, or as cultural artefacts.Texts or cultural artefacts originally in the target language.Academic writing about the texts, the language or the culture. A good range of secondary sources is essential.
Visual ArtsFormal analysis of artworks combined with contextual analysis (historical, social, cultural). Mostly qualitative.Artworks, artists' statements, letters, diaries, interviews.Art history and criticism, exhibition catalogues, scholarly articles.
Biology, Chemistry, PhysicsAn experiment with controlled variables and a standardized method; or analysis of secondary data that used similar standardized approaches and controls.Data you collect in the lab or field.Peer-reviewed publications and databases. ASD expects science EEs to be built on primary (experimental) data — the IB allows secondary-data science essays, but workshop guidance is that they rarely reach the top bands.
Business ManagementA case study of a real organisation, analysed with business tools and concepts.Interviews and surveys (for example with managers, staff or customers), company documents.Academic articles, company reports, industry analysis, quality journalism.
EconomicsApplying economic theory to a real situation and testing it against evidence.Surveys, interviews, prices or data you collect yourself.Data banks and statistics, economic research, news reporting.
Global PoliticsA literature review of key political concepts, then analysis of one or more cases.Speeches, policies, treaties, official documents; your own data where appropriate.Academic writing on the concepts and cases. Primary data may supplement this.
MathematicsTheoretical: exploring mathematics beyond the syllabus. Practical: using mathematics to model or analyse an issue or a data set.Data you collect or obtain (for practical essays).Mathematical texts and articles. A review of the literature is mandatory.
InterdisciplinaryMethods drawn from both of your subjects, chosen so they can be integrated.Whatever counts as primary in each of your two subjects.Secondary research in both subjects.

Not offered at ASD but worth knowing: Psychology extended essays must be based on secondary sources only — collecting primary data is not permitted.

For more on your subject, including the guide's own example research questions and the mistakes to avoid, read your subject guide.

What to do

  1. Read the row for your subject in the table above.
  2. Read two sample essays in your subject: how did each student answer their question, and what did the examiner say about the method?
  3. Find about 10 sources about methods — articles in your topic that show a method you could use, or guides to a method. Save them in PowerNotes in a category called Methods.
