Academic integrity and AI

Your EE has to be your own work, and every reader has to be able to tell your ideas apart from everyone else's. Here is what that means in practice, including how you may and may not use AI.

The guide is direct: if you breach IB rules on academic integrity or ethical research, you put your diploma at risk (guide p. 95).

What readers must be able to trust

  1. The work is yours. Any help you received is acknowledged.
  2. Your words are separate from other people's. Other people's work is shown with citations and entries in your reference list. Word-for-word quotations go inside quotation marks or in an indented block.
  3. Your findings are reported fully and accurately. You never invent data to fit what you expected, never make up a quotation, and never attribute a real quotation to the wrong person.

Help you can and cannot get

Allowed

  • Discussing your ideas with friends, family and teachers.
  • Spelling, grammar and reference-generating software, as long as you check every suggestion yourself.
  • Oral feedback from your supervisor at check-ins and reflection sessions.
  • Written comments from your supervisor on your full draft, once.

Not allowed

  • Collusion: writing any part of your essay together with another student, using another student's work as yours, or letting your work be used in theirs.
  • People, websites or services that edit or write your work for you.
  • Anyone other than you proofreading your essay. Spelling, grammar, formatting and the word count are your job.
  • Written comments from your supervisor on anything except the full draft.

Guide pp. 93–94.

Using AI

Using AI to write your essay and presenting the result as your own is dishonest. The guide does allow AI as a research tool, with conditions (pp. 94–95):

You mayOn condition that
Treat AI-generated material as one of your resourcesYou acknowledge and cite it like any other source, alongside books, articles and websites.
Use AI to help you compare and evaluate other sourcesThe judgements in your essay are still yours.
Ask AI for informationYou check it against other sources. AI can "hallucinate" (invent facts and references), so for the investigation stage the guide recommends ordinary search engines, which list work written by people.

For the full rules, the guide points to Appendix 6 of the IB Academic integrity policy and the IB infographic "The IB and artificial intelligence tools". You will declare how you used AI when you submit your final essay, so keep a record from the start (the thinking tool below).

Habits that keep your work honest

  • Cite while you write. Add the citation the moment you use a source, not when the essay is finished.
  • Use sources to support your ideas, not to say them for you. Your argument comes first; other people's words back it up.
  • Label every note in your RRS: is it your own thought, or someone else's work? If it is someone else's, is it a quotation, a paraphrase or a summary? Record the author, title, page number or URL and date every time.
  • Ask when you are unsure. Your supervisor and the EE coordinator would rather answer a question now than deal with a problem later.

Source: IB Extended essay guide (first assessment 2027), "The ethical researcher": academic integrity, collusion, your own work, your values and skills, using the RRS (pp. 93–95).
