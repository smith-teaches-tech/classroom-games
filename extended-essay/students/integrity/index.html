Academic integrity and AI

Your EE must be your own work. Every reader must be able to tell your ideas apart from other people's. This page shows what that means in practice, including how you may and may not use AI.

The guide is clear: if you break IB rules on academic integrity or ethical research, you put your diploma at risk (guide p. 95).

What readers must be able to trust

  1. The work is yours. Any help you received is acknowledged.
  2. Your words are separate from other people's. You show other people's work with citations and entries in your reference list. Word-for-word quotations go inside quotation marks or in an indented block.
  3. Your findings are reported fully and accurately. You never invent data to fit what you expected, never make up a quotation, and never credit a real quotation to the wrong person.

Help you can and cannot get

Allowed

  • Discussing your ideas with friends, family and teachers.
  • Spelling, grammar and reference-generating software, as long as you check every suggestion yourself.
  • Oral feedback from your supervisor at check-ins and reflection sessions.
  • Written comments from your supervisor on your full draft, once.

Not allowed

  • Collusion: writing any part of your essay together with another student, using another student's work as your own, or letting another student use your work in their essay.
  • People, websites or services that edit or write your work for you.
  • Anyone other than you proofreading your essay. Spelling, grammar, formatting and the word count are your job.
  • Written comments from your supervisor on anything except the full draft.

Guide pp. 93–94.

Using AI

Using AI to write your essay and presenting the result as your own is dishonest. The guide does allow you to use AI as a research tool, with these conditions (pp. 94–95):

You mayOn condition that
Treat AI-generated material as one of your resourcesYou acknowledge and cite the AI material like any other source, alongside books, articles and websites.
Use AI to help you compare and evaluate other sourcesThe judgements in your essay are still your own.
Ask AI for informationYou check the information against other sources. AI can "hallucinate" (invent facts and references). For the investigation stage, the guide recommends ordinary search engines, which list work written by people.

For the full rules, the guide points to Appendix 6 of the IB Academic integrity policy and the IB infographic "The IB and artificial intelligence tools". When you submit your final essay, you'll declare how you used AI, so keep a record from the start (use the thinking tool below).

Habits that keep your work honest

  • Cite while you write. Add the citation as soon as you use a source. Don't wait until the essay is finished.
  • Let sources support your ideas. Your argument comes first, and other people's words back it up.
  • Label every note in your RRS (researcher's reflection space: your own EE notebook). Is the note your own thought, or someone else's work? If the note is someone else's work, is it a quotation, a paraphrase or a summary? Record the author, title, page number or URL, and date every time.
  • Ask when you're unsure. Your supervisor and the EE coordinator would rather answer a question now than deal with a problem later.

Source: IB Extended essay guide (first assessment 2027), "The ethical researcher": academic integrity, collusion, your own work, your values and skills, using the RRS (pp. 93–95).
