What the extended essay is

The extended essay (EE) is a piece of independent research on a question you choose. You investigate it, then write up your findings as a formal academic essay.

4,000 words maximum

Examiners stop reading at 4,000 words, so anything after that point is not marked.

One subject or two

A subject-focused essay uses one DP subject. An interdisciplinary essay combines two DP subjects through one of five frameworks.

Three reflection sessions

You meet your supervisor three times: a first session, an interim session, and a final session called the viva voce.

A 500-word reflective statement

Written after the viva voce on the Reflection and Progress Form (RPF), and marked under Criterion E.

IB examiners mark the essay out of 30 across five criteria (A Framework for the essay, B Knowledge and understanding, C Analysis and line of argument, D Discussion and evaluation, E Reflection). The mark becomes a grade from A to E. Your EE grade and your TOK grade together earn up to three points towards your diploma, and a grade E in the EE is a failing condition.

How the core fits together

CAS

Shows you what you care about — often the best source of an EE topic.

TOK

Gives you the questioning: what counts as evidence in a subject, and which methods it trusts.

Your subjects

Give you the knowledge, concepts and terminology.

The EE

Is where you put all three together, on your own question.

How this site works

There are 19 steps in four stages: Explore, Narrow, Research and write, and After submission. Do them in order. Each step page has:

  • Why this step matters and what to do, in a short guide.
  • A thinking tool — questions that help you brainstorm. Nothing is submitted or graded. Your answers save in your browser, and a button copies them into your RRS Google Doc.
  • Before you move on — a short checklist at the end of the thinking tool.

Your extended essay is your own independent work. The thinking tools only ask questions; they never write anything for you.

Set up your RRS before anything else

The IB strongly recommends that every EE student keeps a researcher's reflection space (RRS): a place to record what you read, what you think, the questions that come up, and the decisions you make. You will use your RRS to prepare for each reflection session, and it is the best source for your reflective statement at the end.

  1. Create a Google Doc called EE RRS — your name.
  2. Add a heading for today's date, then copy the thinking tool below into the Google Doc with the Copy for Google Docs button.
  3. Do the same at every step. Add your own notes in between — reactions to sources, new questions, things you changed your mind about.

Source: IB Extended essay guide (first assessment 2027), "Reflection and the extended essay" and "Assessment".
