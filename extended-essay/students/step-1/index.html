Classroom tools/Extended Essay/For Students/Step 1

Choose a subject and a broad topic

Start with something you genuinely care about, then decide which subject — or which two subjects — will study it.

Why this step matters

You will live with this topic for more than a year. The IB guide calls choosing a topic "arguably the most important stage" of the extended essay, because it drives everything that follows.

At this stage you only need a broad topic — an area such as "sport and national identity" or "water use in desert cities". You will narrow it into a research question later, in steps 8 to 10.

One topic, several subjects

The same broad topic can become very different essays depending on the subject that studies it. The IB guide uses sport as an example:

SubjectHow that subject might approach sport
Global PoliticsExplore global discourses around women in sport and how these influence political decisions about gender rights.
Business ManagementAnalyse a case study of a brand sponsoring a football club.

What makes an essay belong to a subject is not the content but the concepts, methods and theories of that subject that you use to investigate it.

Choose your pathway

Subject-focused pathway

One DP subject. The IB strongly recommends a subject you are already studying in the DP, so you can build on concepts and methods you know.

Interdisciplinary pathway

Two DP subjects combined, positioned within one of the five interdisciplinary frameworks. The IB strongly recommends that you study at least one of the two subjects.

The five interdisciplinary frameworks, with some of the IB's suggested topic areas:

  • Power, equality, justice — health, education, racism, gender, poverty, fairness, conflict.
  • Culture, identity, expression — belief systems, the politics of identity, sporting competitions, art and science, music, protests, cultural heritage, gaming.
  • Movement, time, space — migration, refugees, virtual communities, astronomy, urbanism, social media, globalization, museums.
  • Evidence, measurement, innovation — art and science, literature and science, mathematics in science, gaming, social media.
  • Sustainability, development, change — environmental, social and economic sustainability; economic and social development; food; health; poverty.

Your topic only needs to fit one framework broadly, and it does not need to touch every word in the framework's name. The two subjects do not need an even 50/50 balance — the balance depends on your research question.

Rule: Environmental Systems and Societies (ESS) and Literature and Performance are "cross-disciplinary" subjects. You can write a subject-focused essay in either one, but you cannot use either one in the interdisciplinary pathway.

Rules to know for your subject

Before you hand in your subject choice, read the subject guide for your first choice and your backup subject. Each guide sets out what the IB expects in that subject, with example research questions and a thinking tool to test your idea.

  • Language A: the essay must be based on one or more clearly defined texts, and at least one text must have been originally written in the language of the essay. You cannot base it on a text studied as part of your language and literature course. Avoid biographical, historical or sociological topics — the focus must be literary or language analysis.
  • Language B: you cannot write it in a language you study as Language A. Every text you analyse must have been originally written or spoken in the target language — translations are not accepted.
  • Visual Arts: the essay must stay on artistic meaning and analysis; do not drift into psychology, history or sociology, or treat artworks as documentary evidence.
  • Mathematics: the essay can be theoretical (mathematics beyond the syllabus) or practical (using mathematics to investigate an issue or data). A review of the literature is always required.
  • Every subject: your EE must not duplicate any other work you submit for the diploma, such as an internal assessment (IA).

Check: is this really your subject?

Before you go further, make sure the topic belongs to the subject you are choosing. Ask yourself which concepts, theories and methods from that subject you would use. If you cannot name any, the topic may belong to a different subject. Changing subject later costs you time and may involve a fee, so talk to the EE coordinator if you are unsure.

Source: IB Extended essay guide (first assessment 2027), "Overview of extended essay pathways", "Generic guidance" and "Subject-specific guidance".
