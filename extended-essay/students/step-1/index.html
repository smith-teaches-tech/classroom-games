Classroom tools/Extended Essay/For Students/Step 1

Choose a subject and a broad topic

Start with something you care about. Then decide which subject (or which two subjects) you'll use to study it.

Why this step matters

You'll work on this topic for more than a year. The IB guide calls choosing a topic "arguably the most important stage" of the extended essay. Your topic shapes everything that follows.

For now you only need a broad topic: an area such as "sport and national identity" or "water use in desert cities". You'll narrow your broad topic into a research question later, in steps 8 to 10.

One topic, several subjects

The same broad topic can become very different essays, depending on which subject you use to study it. The IB guide uses sport as an example:

SubjectHow that subject might approach sport
Global PoliticsExplore global discourses (the ways people talk and write) about women in sport, and how these discourses influence political decisions about gender rights.
Business ManagementAnalyse a case study of a brand sponsoring a football club.

An essay belongs to a subject when you use that subject's concepts, methods and theories to investigate the topic.

Choose your pathway

Subject-focused pathway

One DP subject. The IB strongly recommends a subject you already study in the DP, so you can build on concepts and methods you know.

Interdisciplinary pathway

Two DP subjects combined, placed within one of the five interdisciplinary frameworks (broad themes set by the IB). The IB strongly recommends that you study at least one of the two subjects.

The five interdisciplinary frameworks, with some of the IB's suggested topic areas:

  • Power, equality, justice: health, education, racism, gender, poverty, fairness, conflict.
  • Culture, identity, expression: belief systems, the politics of identity, sporting competitions, art and science, music, protests, cultural heritage, gaming.
  • Movement, time, space: migration, refugees, virtual communities, astronomy, urbanism, social media, globalization, museums.
  • Evidence, measurement, innovation: art and science, literature and science, mathematics in science, gaming, social media.
  • Sustainability, development, change: environmental, social and economic sustainability; economic and social development; food; health; poverty.

Your topic only needs to fit one framework broadly. It doesn't need to cover every word in the framework's name. The two subjects don't need an even 50/50 balance. The balance depends on your research question.

Rule: Environmental Systems and Societies (ESS) and Literature and Performance are "cross-disciplinary" subjects. You can write a subject-focused essay in either subject. You can't use either one in the interdisciplinary pathway.

Rules to know for your subject

Before you hand in your subject choice, read the subject guide for your first-choice subject and your backup subject. Each subject guide explains what the IB expects in that subject. It also has example research questions and a thinking tool to test your idea.

  • Language A: the essay must be based on one or more clearly defined texts. At least one text must have been originally written in the language of your essay. You can't base the essay on a text you studied in your language and literature course. Avoid biographical, historical or sociological topics: the focus must be literary or language analysis.
  • Language B: you can't write a Language B essay in a language you study as Language A. Every text you analyse must have been originally written or spoken in the target language (the language you're learning). Translations are not accepted.
  • Visual Arts: the essay must stay focused on artistic meaning and analysis. Don't drift into psychology, history or sociology, and don't treat artworks as documentary evidence (records of what happened).
  • Mathematics: the essay can be theoretical (mathematics beyond the syllabus) or practical (using mathematics to investigate an issue or data). A review of the literature (what has already been written on your topic) is always required.
  • Every subject: your EE must not repeat any other work you submit for the diploma, such as an internal assessment (IA).

Check: is this really your subject?

Before you go further, make sure your topic belongs to the subject you're choosing. Ask yourself which concepts, theories and methods from that subject you would use. If you can't name any, your topic may belong to a different subject. Changing subject later costs you time and may involve a fee. Talk to the EE coordinator if you're unsure.

Source: IB Extended essay guide (first assessment 2027), "Overview of extended essay pathways", "Generic guidance" and "Subject-specific guidance".
