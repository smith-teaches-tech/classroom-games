Classroom tools/Extended Essay/For Students/Step 8

Narrow your topic and write a broad question

Turn what you learned from your sources into a focused topic, then into a broad question you can test.

Why this step matters

A broad topic leads to a descriptive essay that informs the reader but never argues anything. The IB guide warns that staying "too broad and general almost inevitably leads to descriptive, superficial writing". Narrowing is how you get to analysis.

You are not writing your final research question yet — that comes in step 10. Here you narrow the topic and write a broad question: a working question that will guide which sources you annotate in step 9.

The topic funnel

Narrow one level at a time. Each level should be a part of the level above it.

LevelExample (Economics)Example (Language A)
Broad topicGovernment intervention in marketsDystopian fiction
Narrower aspectPrice controlsHow setting creates fear in dystopian novels
A specific case, text, place or timeMinimum unit pricing of alcohol in Scotland since 2018Setting in an Orwell novel
Focused topicWhether minimum unit pricing reduced alcohol consumption in ScotlandHow Orwell uses setting to show the dangers of a totalitarian society

Both examples are based on sample essays in the sample library (Economics Sample B and Language A Sample B).

Check your focused topic

The IB guide suggests a preliminary investigation of your focused topic. You have already done most of it in steps 3 to 7. Ask yourself:

  • What has already been written about this topic?
  • Is it easy to find sources of information?
  • Is there a range of different resources available?
  • Is there a range of views or perspectives on the topic?
  • What interesting questions have started to emerge from my reading?

Brainstorm, then evaluate

  1. Write at least five open-ended questions about your focused topic. The IB guide recommends starting them with how, why or to what extent.
  2. Judge each question against three tests from the guide: is it clear, is it focused, and is it arguable (could reasonable people answer it differently)?
  3. Choose the strongest one as your broad question. It is allowed — expected — to change.

Changing direction is normal. The IB guide says a change of topic or question "is completely acceptable and will be supported by your supervisor". Sometimes a slight change of wording is all that is needed.

Source: IB Extended essay guide (first assessment 2027), "Generic guidance" (choosing a topic) and "Researching the extended essay" (four steps to developing a research question; changing direction).
