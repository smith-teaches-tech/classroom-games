Ethics check

Before you collect any data, check that your research is safe and fair to everyone involved. Then talk it through with your supervisor.

Why this step matters

If you break the IB's rules on ethical research, you put your diploma at risk. If you check now, you won't have to change your plan later. (guide p. 95)

Five things to check

  • People: if anyone answers questions or takes part, they must agree first (this is called informed consent). Keep their names private. Don't make anyone uncomfortable.
  • Animals and living things: follow your teacher's and the IB's rules.
  • Safety: chemicals, equipment and fieldwork need a risk assessment.
  • The environment: don't damage habitats, and don't waste materials.
  • Sensitive topics: if your topic could upset you or others, talk to your supervisor first.

In your subject

Biology, Chemistry, Physics: Do a risk assessment with your teacher before any lab or field work. Follow the IB's science experiment rules for animals, dissection, human experiments, microbes, and chemicals and waste. Don't choose a question where getting enough data would be unethical. (guide pp. 64, 96)
Business Management: Interviews and surveys need consent. Keep people's names private. Ask before you use a company's internal documents, and keep confidential information confidential.
Global Politics: Interviews need consent, and some people may want to stay anonymous. Political topics can be sensitive: talk to your supervisor about how to approach yours.
Psychology: You don't collect data, so you have no participants. If your topic is sensitive (for example, mental health), talk to your supervisor about how to write about it with care.
Language A: Most Language A essays involve no participants. If your topic is sensitive, talk to your supervisor.
Language B: The guide warns against questionnaires you write yourself: published sources are more reliable. If you do survey people, you need their consent. (guide p. 49)
Visual Arts: If you interview an artist or gallery staff, ask for consent and permission to quote them.
Mathematics: If you collect data from people, you need their consent, and the data must not show who they are.
Interdisciplinary: Check the rules for both of your subjects. Ask your supervisor which ones apply.
