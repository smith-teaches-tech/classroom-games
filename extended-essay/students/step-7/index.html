Classroom tools/Extended Essay/For Students/Step 7

Collect your sources in PowerNotes

Bring your reading together: enough sources for your subject, organised so you can see what your topic is really about.

Why this step matters

A wide collection of sources shows you the shape of your topic: the themes that keep coming back, the arguments that matter, and the gaps. You'll probably collect more sources than you cite. That's normal. In step 9 you'll choose the most useful sources and annotate them (write a short note that sums up and judges each one).

How many sources?

The IB doesn't set a number of sources. Different subjects need very different amounts of reading, so the targets below depend on your subject. The targets come from an analysis of strong EEs in each subject group: the bibliographies of the IB's sample essays that earned a B or higher.

SubjectCollect (step 7)Annotate (step 9)What counts
Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Business Management, Economics, Global Politics, interdisciplinary essays2512Scholarly articles, books, reports and data.
Visual Arts2010Includes the 3 or 4 artworks your essay analyses (your primary sources).
Language A, Language B158Includes your primary text or texts: the novel, play, poems, films or songs you analyse.
Mathematics106Textbooks, papers and reliable mathematical references.

Your subject from step 1 is highlighted.

  • Language A and Language B: your novel, play, poems, films or songs are your primary sources. Reading your primary texts closely, more than once, is the biggest part of your research. That's why you need fewer other sources. Record your primary texts in PowerNotes too. They count towards your total.
  • Visual Arts: the artworks you analyse are your primary sources, and they count towards your total.
  • More is not better. In the sample essays, the number of sources did not predict the mark. A Physics essay with 30 sources scored 11 out of 30. A Language B essay with about 15 sources scored 23. What matters is how well you use each source.

Organise PowerNotes

You already have four categories from steps 3 to 6. Keep them, and add a new category for each theme as patterns appear. Language A, Language B and Visual Arts students: add a fifth category for the works you'll analyse, called "Primary texts" or "Artworks".

Open / background

From step 3. Background and key terms.

Current

From step 4. Recent research and debate.

Historical

From step 5. Foundational work.

Methods

From step 6. How your question could be answered.

For every source, check that PowerNotes has a full citation in your chosen style. Add a one-line note on why the source matters.

Check your collection

  • Spread: no single category should hold more than about half your sources.
  • Quality: a good share of your sources should be scholarly: peer-reviewed articles (checked by other experts before publication), academic books, or data from reputable organisations.
  • Perspectives: do your sources disagree with each other anywhere? If every source says the same thing, look for the other side.
  • Records: every source has a complete citation in your chosen style.

Then step back

Read your one-line notes from top to bottom. Which themes keep coming up? Which sources do you keep going back to? Where do you feel most curious? Your answers point to the narrower topic you'll choose in step 8.
