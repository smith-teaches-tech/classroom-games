For Teachers

Your role and key dates

The essentials for EE supervisors. About a five-minute read.

Your job in one line: you guide the process with questions. The student does the research and the writing. You don't need to be an expert in the topic. (guide p. 103)

What you must do

  • Hold three reflection sessions (20 to 30 minutes each) and record them on the RPF. See The three reflection sessions.
  • Be available for short check-ins between sessions.
  • Help the student shape a focused research question and choose methods.
  • Check ethics and safety before the student collects any data.
  • Read and comment on one full draft. Only one.
  • Read the final essay, hold the viva voce, and confirm the essay is the student's own work.
  • Send a predicted grade.

Time: the IB recommends 3 to 5 hours per student in total, including the three sessions. (guide pp. 104–105)

Feedback: what you can and can't do

You can

  • Talk through ideas and ask questions.
  • Point to resources, people and the guide.
  • Give oral feedback on the outline and the introduction.
  • Write open comments on the one full draft.

You can't

  • Correct spelling or punctuation.
  • Correct experiments or mathematics.
  • Rewrite any part of the essay.
  • Say where sections should move.
  • Proofread.
  • Correct citations or the bibliography.

At ASD, all feedback before the full draft is oral. (EE support material p. 20)

Commenting on the full draft

Ask questions that point to the problem. Don't fix it. Then go through your comments with the student in person.

If you see…You could write…
Unclear argument"Your essay lacks clarity here. How could you make it clearer?"
A calculation error"Check this page carefully."
A missing section"There is something missing here. What is it? Check the essay against the requirements."
Key material in the appendix"Are you sure this belongs here?"
A weak conclusion"What is it that you are trying to say here? Have you included all your relevant findings? Have you looked at unanswered questions?"
A citation problem"You need to check this page for accuracy of referencing."

After the draft, the next version you see is the final essay. It must be a clean copy with no comments. Once you've discussed it with the student, it can't be changed. (guide p. 108)

If something doesn't feel right

  • If you doubt the work is the student's own, talk to the student first.
  • Useful checks: compare the essay with the student's other writing and with the draft, look up a few of the sources, and ask the student to explain a section.
  • Then tell the EE coordinator. An essay is not uploaded while its authenticity is in doubt. (guide p. 106)
