For Teachers

The three reflection sessions

20 to 30 minutes each. The student talks, you ask questions. Pick three or four questions; you don't need them all.

After every session: on the RPF, write the month, the student's DP year (1 or 2) and your initials. That's all you write on the form.

Check-ins between sessions are short and informal. They aren't recorded on the RPF. (guide pp. 97–98; RPF)

1. First reflection session

At ASD:

Student brings
A draft research question, the annotated bibliography and their RRS (researcher's reflection space: their notebook or doc).
Aim
A working research question, a research plan, and any ethical issues flagged.
Next
Research question approved by .

Questions to ask

  • What made you choose this topic?
  • What have you read so far? What did it tell you?
  • What is your working research question? Can you answer it in 4,000 words?
  • Which methods will you use, and why?
  • Are there any ethical or safety issues with your topic or methods?
  • What will you do before we next meet?

2. Interim reflection session

At ASD:

Student brings
Their introduction and literature review, their data or evidence so far, and their RRS.
Aim
Check the direction of the essay. Give oral feedback on the introduction.
Next
Full draft due . Your written comments on the draft are the only written feedback.

Questions to ask

  • Where are you now in your research?
  • What has been hard, and what did you do about it?
  • How has your understanding of the question changed?
  • Is your research question still clear and focused?
  • Do you have enough evidence to support your argument?
  • What needs to happen before the full draft?

3. Viva voce (final reflection session)

At ASD: , after the final essay is handed in on

Before
Read the final essay. The essay can't be changed after this session.
Aim
Celebrate the finished essay, confirm the ideas are the student's own, and give the student material for their reflective statement.
Next
The student writes the 500-word reflective statement in class by .

Questions to ask

  • How did you arrive at your research question?
  • Talk me through one source or result in your essay. Why does it matter to your argument?
  • Show me an entry in your RRS where your thinking changed.
  • What were your successes? What would you do differently?
  • What skills from the EE will you use again, at university or in work?
