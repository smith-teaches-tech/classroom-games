RPF & Criterion E

What the RPF is

The Reflection and Progress Form is a two-page form submitted with every extended essay.

  • 1
    Page 1: three reflection sessions. The supervisor records the month, DP year and their initials after each one: first session, interim session, and the final viva voce.
  • 2
    Page 2: the reflective statement. Written by the student after the viva voce. No more than 500 words. This is the only part marked, under Criterion E.

Criterion E gets 0 if the RPF is blank or the statement is not in the language of the essay.

More detail
  • The RPF is mandatory and is submitted together with the completed essay.
  • The first session covers initial ideas and the research plan; the interim session comes once a significant amount of research is done; the viva voce comes after the essay is handed in.
  • Other check-ins are allowed but are not recorded on the form.
  • The form supports the authenticity of the work. By submitting the essay, the supervisor takes responsibility for its authenticity; work in doubt should not be uploaded.

What the rubric asks

Does the student evaluate the effect of the extended essay learning experience on them as a learner?

4

Consistently evaluative, with specific examples. Growth and transfer shown throughout.

3

Partially evaluative, with specific examples. Growth and transfer shown often.

2

Descriptive, with some specific examples. Growth and transfer shown sometimes.

1

Descriptive. Only describes the process; no transfer of learning.

Full rubric wording
1 mark2 marks3 marks4 marks
Evaluative Reflection on the extended essay learning experience is descriptive. Reflection on the extended essay learning experience is descriptive and includes some specific examples. Reflection on the extended essay learning experience is partially evaluative and includes specific examples. Reflection on the extended essay learning experience is consistently evaluative and includes specific examples.
Growth Reflection on the student's growth is limited to a description of the process and there is no evidence of transfer of learning. Reflection sometimes shows evidence of the student's growth and transfer of learning. Reflection often shows evidence of the student's growth and transfer of learning. Reflection consistently shows evidence of the student's growth and transfer of learning.

Evidence may be drawn from, but is not limited to:

  • experiences and insights that could shape future thinking
  • explicit examples of how skills learned could be used in other current contexts and in the future
  • how changes in perspective impacted decision-making.

Work that does not reach the 1-mark descriptors receives 0. Source: IB Extended essay guide, Criterion E.

Samples with scores

Six reflective statements from IB sample RPFs.

Patterns across the six samples
  • "Why", not just "what". The Biology statement explains why its scope had to narrow. The Business statement reports changes without saying what they taught.
  • Length is not quality. The Mathematics statement uses about 495 words and scores 2. The Interdisciplinary statement uses about 300 and scores 3.
  • Named moments earn marks. A surprising result (Biology), feedback on a draft (Language A), a draft that failed (Interdisciplinary).
  • Transfer has to be shown. Saying skills will help "in my future job" (Language A) is not the same as giving an example.
